# Fact-Check and Argument Evidence Research

Use this mode for technical or public-affairs articles, timelines, quotations, statistics, attribution, and causal claims.

## Claim Decomposition

- Split the target into atomic claims and classify each as fact, quotation, estimate, prediction, causal inference, opinion, or value judgment
- Record who made the claim, the event date, publication date, jurisdiction, population, units, denominator, and comparison baseline where relevant
- Trace quotations to the original speech, transcript, filing, dataset, paper, or first-party document and inspect surrounding context

## Corroboration

- Prefer original records and datasets; use reputable independent reporting to cross-check chronology and contested interpretation
- Seek evidence that could falsify the claim, not only sources that repeat it
- Distinguish multiple reports based on one common source from genuinely independent confirmation
- For causal claims, test temporal order, plausible mechanism, confounders, selection effects, and alternative explanations

## Verdict Vocabulary

Use bounded labels tied to evidence, such as supported, partly supported, contradicted, misleading by omission/context, unverifiable with available evidence, or opinion/prediction rather than fact. Explain the basis; do not reduce nuanced evidence to a label alone.

## Failure Patterns

- Treating headlines, snippets, virality, or repeated secondary reports as independent evidence
- Confusing reporting date with event date
- Using absence of evidence as proof of absence
- Calling a claim false when the available evidence only shows that it is unverified
